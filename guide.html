Your first battle

How to Play, opened from the title screen, begins with Training. Basic Training's five lessons are open from the start; each is a small battle on the Fall Weiss map, the first battle of the war, and every step is checked by what you actually do. The enemy never moves in a lesson, and it ends when its last step is done. Finish them all for the Basic Training award.

1. First Moves

Select a unit, move it, use the roads, end the turn.

  1. Your units are the counters without a dark pennant. Tap the Panzer II to select it.
  2. The bright line shows where it can go this turn. Tap a hex inside it to move there.
  3. Roads are fast in any weather. Select the infantry and move it onto the road.
  4. When you have done all you want, tap End Turn. In a battle the enemy then moves; in training it stands still.

2. Attacking

Read the forecast, attack, and wear an enemy down.

  1. Select your Panzer III, then tap the enemy outlined beside it. The forecast shows what each side will likely lose.
  2. Tap Attack to go through with it.
  3. A unit attacked again in the same turn defends worse. Attack the same enemy with your infantry.
  4. Each unit may move once and attack once a turn. Tap End Turn.

3. Objectives

Take ringed towns, and read the orders.

  1. Towns ringed in the enemy's colour are objectives. Move the Panzer II into Lodz to take it.
  2. Take Poznan with the infantry too.
  3. Tap OBJ at the top to see every objective, and what a decisive victory needs.
  4. Take them all by the decisive turn for a decisive victory. Tap End Turn.

4. Ground and Digging In

Use forest and towns, and dig in.

  1. Forest, towns and mountains protect whoever stands in them. Move the infantry into the forest beside it.
  2. A unit that has not moved can dig in for more protection. Select the artillery and tap Dig In.
  3. Tap End Turn.

5. Supply and Refit

See your supply, and bring a worn unit back to strength.

  1. Supply keeps an army fighting. Open the Supply view with the switch under the status bar.
  2. A worn unit in supply and away from the enemy can refit. Select the damaged Panzer III and tap Refit.
  3. Tap End Turn.

Know Your Units

A turn with each kind of unit, doing what it does best: Infantry (cheap and stubborn: it takes towns and holds them), Armour (fast, and hits hard in the open), Recon (the fastest unit, and the one that sees furthest), Anti-Tank Guns (made to kill tanks), Artillery (fires from afar, where the target cannot answer), Flak (guards the units around it from aircraft), Fighters (hunt enemy aircraft) and Bombers (smash ground units from the air).

Advanced Training

Open from the start too: Reading the Duel (follow an attack card by card and die by die), Flanking and Overwatch (take an enemy from both sides, then set a gun in ambush), Closing a Ring (seal two enemy units off from their supply), The Air War (strike through flak, and see the ground it covers) and Calling Up Units (bring a new unit into the battle).

Each of the Field Manual's ten chapters, once your commander has learned it on a posting, opens a lesson of its own: Tempo, Sieges, Fighting the Weather, Cutting Supply, The Expert's Habits, Suppression, Fighting Armour, Fighting Infantry, Guns and Aircraft.

Then start a campaign. The first battle's tips walk you through it, and before turn 1 you may deploy: tap a unit, then a shaded hex to move it there or another of your units to swap them.

The turn

Select a unit, move it, attack with it, then End Turn: the enemy answers, and the next turn is yours.

Tap one of your units to select it. A bright line shows where it can reach; tap a hex inside it to move.

Tap an enemy in range to see the odds, then tap again to attack. Each unit may move once and attack once.

When you have done all you want, End Turn. The enemy moves, and a Report from the Front sums up its turn: what it attacked and what it cost you. Watch Turn shows it move by move, the camera following each action close up (Settings can turn that off); Continue goes straight to your turn. Read the last report again from the ☰ menu.

Before the first turn you may deploy: tap a unit, then a shaded hex to move it there or another of your units to swap them. Begin Battle when your army is set.

Touch and hold any hex, or tap an enemy, to see the ground and the unit on it. Tap OBJ at the top for the objectives and what each victory needs.

A unit’s card lists everything in force on it under In Force: the weather as its type feels it, the ground, digging in, tactics, wear from attacks, and supply trouble. Small symbols beside its name in the bar show the same at a glance, trouble first, each coloured by whether it helps or hurts you.

Drag to move the map and pinch to zoom. The map can be dragged a little past its edges, so units in the corners come out from under the buttons; a unit you select near an edge slides into view by itself.

Winning

Take the ringed objectives, and hold them, before the battle’s last turn.

Most battles: take every ringed objective by the end of the last turn. Taking them all by the decisive turn earns a decisive victory, which opens the better branch of the campaign.

Defensive battles: you start holding the objectives and must still hold enough of them when time runs out.

Some battles are won by destroying the enemy force. The briefing always says which.

Holding what you took: a decisive victory needs every objective held through two of the enemy’s turns after the last one falls (or to the battle’s end). Taken after the decisive turn, it is a victory at once. The same holds for the enemy in a defence.

Movement

Roads are fast, rough ground is slow, and moving next to an enemy ends a move.

Roads are fast in any weather. Forest, rivers and mountains are slow; armour cannot enter mountains or marsh.

Moving next to an enemy ends your move. You may pass through your own units but not stop on them.

Bad weather slows most units off the roads, vehicles and guns most of all; infantry walks through rain. Weather has a page of its own.

Combat

Attack against defence sets how likely each die is to hit; ground, digging in and repeated attacks shift the odds.

Attack strength against defence decides how likely each die is to hit. Most fights are three dice a side; the air war changes that for aircraft (see Air war). Soft targets (everything but armour) and hard targets (armour) are hit with different attack values.

A unit fights with the strength it has left: at half strength, half its attack and half its defence.

Terrain protects: towns, forts, forest and mountains all raise defence. A unit that digs in gains more.

A unit attacked several times in one turn defends worse each time. Soften a position with artillery and aircraft, then send in the tanks, then the infantry.

Fire from beyond the target’s reach is not returned. Artillery within two hexes also supports its neighbours in attack and defence.

A ground unit that destroys an enemy beside it may take its ground: tap the arrow before you end the turn to move in (and take the town there), or leave it where it stands.

Units and their jobs

Every type of unit has a job: win by using each for what it does best.

A unit’s numbers: soft attack is its fire at everything but armour, hard attack its fire at armour, and air attack its fire at aircraft. Defence protects it; an aircraft defends with its air defence. Initiative decides who fires first, range how far it can fire, movement how far it goes. Tap any unit, on the map or in a list of units to buy, for its numbers.

Infantry: cheap and stubborn, the best at holding towns, forests and mountains, and the only units that can bridge a river. Weak against armour in the open.

Armour: fast, and hits soft and hard targets alike. It overruns battered infantry and guns. Keep it out of forests and towns, where infantry and anti-tank guns wait for it; it cannot enter mountains or marsh.

Recon: the fastest ground unit, and the one that sees furthest. Use it to find the enemy under fog of war, not to fight.

Anti-tank guns: the answer to armour, with a hard attack most tanks cannot match. Best dug in where the tanks must come, or waiting on Overwatch.

Artillery: fires from two or three hexes away, where the target cannot answer, and supports the units beside it in attack and defence. Weak if anything reaches it: keep it behind the line.

Flak (anti-aircraft guns): fires at enemy aircraft that attack any unit beside it (see Air war). Stand it beside your artillery, your towns and your best units.

Fighters: hunt aircraft, with 4 dice when they attack one, and are the best guard against bombers. Poor against ground units.

Bombers (dive bombers and heavy bombers): strike ground units with 4 dice. They fear fighters and flak. Mud and storms ground every aircraft away from a city’s runways.

Prestige

Prestige is High Command’s regard for you, and what you pay for almost everything.

Earned: an eighth of an enemy unit’s price when you destroy it; 50 for taking an objective and 20 for any other town; 5 at the start of each of your turns for every town or airfield you hold and 15 for every objective, from the second turn on; and the battle’s award when you win, more for a decisive victory.

Also: 25 for every enemy unit that surrenders in a pocket, 100 for destroying a Bloodied unit, prestige for opening a corridor into a pocket, and each optional goal’s own reward.

Spent in battle: calling up units, resting or refitting a worn unit, and intelligence from the Intel view.

Spent at Headquarters: new units, veterans to refill a unit without losing its grade, upgrades to newer equipment, the care of each building, and better rations.

In a campaign, what you spend in the field is spent, but of the towns’ pay still unspent at a battle’s end only half goes back to Headquarters. Hard pays less for a victory. Battle Mode spends Command Points instead.

Difficulty

Difficulty changes how well the enemy plays and what it fields. It never cheats.

The computer sees only what you see and rolls the same dice. What changes is how well it plans, and handicaps the setup screen names.

Easy: The enemy fields four-fifths of its strength, attacks piecemeal, makes more mistakes and only calls up units to replace its losses.

Normal: The battles as written. The enemy masses before it attacks, concentrates its fire and may call up two units more than it began with.

Hard: Every enemy unit is a grade more experienced, its garrisons start dug in when it defends, the enemy plans every attack against the clock and may call up five units more than it began with, victories pay less prestige, and fog of war is always on.

Fog of war is off to begin with on Easy and on to begin with on Normal. The enemy commander is about half your level on Easy, level with you on Normal, and ahead of you on Hard.

A campaign’s score counts the difficulty: Easy pays about a third, Hard double.

Controls

The Siri Remote: swipe or press the edges of the clickpad to move the cursor a hex at a time; click to select a unit, move it or attack. Click the selected unit again for its orders: Refit, Dig In, Details.

Back lets go of a selection or closes a card; with nothing selected it opens the battle menu, where End Turn and every other order are. Play/Pause selects the next unit that has not moved.

On menus and cards, swipe to move between buttons and click to choose.

Tap a unit to select it, a hex to move there, and an enemy to see the odds; tap again to attack. Touch and hold any hex for its ground and the unit on it.

Drag to move the map and pinch to zoom; the zoom buttons at the foot of the screen do the same. Next selects the next unit that has not moved.

The duel

At Normal speed every attack is fought out between the two units’ cards, with the battle’s own dice.

At Normal battle speed (Settings) every attack, yours and the enemy’s, is played out between the two units’ cards with the dice in the middle. Quick plays attacks on the map only.

Each side throws three dice, unless the air war says otherwise (see Air war). Each die is a number from 1 to 100, and it hits when it comes up at or under the number the odds give: 80, 65, 50, 35, 20 or 10, from how the attack compares with the defence. Every hit costs a strength point. These are the battle’s own dice, shown as they fell.

The attacker throws first and the defender answers with what it has left, unless it has the higher initiative and fires first. A unit fired on from beyond its range does not answer, and nor does one with no weapon against its attacker (a tank against a Stuka). A die that can destroy a unit rolls slowly.

Flak fires before the strike, in a row of its own at the top. Each hit takes a strength point off the aircraft; an aircraft shot down never strikes, and its card is stamped Shot Down.

The cards show what each side fights with, every term of the sum, its strength falling as the dice land, its battles and enemy destroyed, its medals, ribbons and traits, and any Nemesis or Bloodied mark. Turn a card over (the arrows in its corner) for its record: its road of battles, its medals and where they were won, this battle’s moments. The duel waits while a card is turned.

Tap to skip to the result; tap again, or Carry On, to go on. The attack’s rare moments are told on the cards, and the big ones still get their banner after: a rare one its close-up, a legendary one the whole screen.

On the enemy’s turn the map shows the move first, then a red arrow from the attacker to its target with the odds, then the duel.

Overrun: armour that attacks infantry or anti-tank guns and leaves them with 3 strength or less, while it still has more strength than they do, rolls straight on and throws one more die at the same odds (three more in a Scenario match). The defender cannot answer it. If the overrun leaves it with 2 or less it is thrown back.

Manoeuvre

Surround, flank and wait in ambush: it beats hammering a position from the front.

Flanking: an attack gains +1 for each other unit of yours already beside the defender, up to +3. Move units up around a target before the blow lands.

A defender with your units on opposite sides of it is Flanked: −2 defence, and it cannot fall back. A blow that would have pushed it back costs it 2 strength where it stands instead.

Rivers: a ground unit attacking out of a river hex fights at −3. A bridge (a road the river runs through) crosses without it.

A unit beside a bridge can blow it as its whole turn: the road is gone and the river runs on. Infantry can bridge a river in a turn from beside it, if no enemy stands on the far bank; engineers can do it even after a short move.

Overwatch: a unit that has not moved or fired can wait instead. In the enemy’s turn it fires once, at the usual odds, at the first enemy that moves into its range. Destroyed or thrown back, the mover stops there. Guns and anti-tank units make the most of it.

Digging in gives +4 at once, then +1 for each of its turns a unit is not attacked, up to +8. Each attack it takes wears off 1. A number on the counter shows anything past +4. Flanking and wearing a position down beat hammering it from the front.

Air war

Flak and held cities fire at aircraft before they strike, and each kind of unit throws its own dice against aircraft.

Flak covers the six hexes around it. When an enemy aircraft attacks a unit next to an anti-aircraft gun (the 2cm Flak 38, Wirbelwind, Bofors, 3.7-inch AA, 40mm M1, M16, 61-K, ZSU-37 and the 88mm FlaK), the gun fires 1 die at the aircraft before it strikes; 2 dice from a city. Only the strongest gun beside the target fires.

A held city has guns of its own: an aircraft attacking a unit in a city its side holds meets 1 die of the city’s fire first, besides any flak.

Each hit takes a strength point off the aircraft before it strikes, so it hits less hard. An aircraft shot down never strikes at all, and the gun that brought it down gets the kill.

In a fight of its own, who throws how many dice: flak 3 at aircraft (4 from a city), when it attacks one or one attacks it; infantry 1 at aircraft; fighters 2 against ground units, 4 attacking aircraft and 3 when an aircraft attacks them; bombers 4 attacking ground units and 2 attacking aircraft. Every other fight is three a side. A unit with no air attack (most tanks and guns) cannot fire at aircraft at all.

Select an aircraft and the ground the enemy’s flak covers is washed red, with the enemy’s cities; select one of your flak guns and the hexes it covers are outlined. The odds before an attack say what flak waits, and count it.

These rules are in every battle against the computer and in Battle Mode. A Scenario match plays without them.

Morale

Every unit has morale: it rises and falls when a fight goes better or worse than its odds, and it spreads.

Every unit has morale, aircraft too: Broken, Shaken, Steady, Confident or Inspired. Each step above Steady adds a point of attack and defence; each below takes one away. A Broken unit cannot attack.

Nothing predicts it. Morale moves only when a fight goes clearly better or worse than its odds: hit harder and lose less than expected, and a unit rises a step while the enemy it fought falls one. Driven out of a town, a unit falls a step.

It spreads. A surprise touches the units beside it half the time, the same way. And at the start of its turn a unit drifts a step toward its neighbours when most of them stand above or below it. The forecast counts a unit’s morale as it is now.

At the ends of the scale a unit may gain a trait, by chance. Reaching Inspired: Leader (the units beside it never fall below Shaken) or Careless (−2 defence, and it will not dig in). Reaching Broken: Unbreakable (never below Shaken again, +1 defence) or Fearful (−1 attack, and it starts each battle Shaken).

Traits stay with a unit like medals; a unit holds one from each end, and a new one replaces the old. Fearful lifts after a battle won without losing a unit; Careless lifts when a hard knock leaves it Shaken or worse. In a campaign, a unit that ended a battle Broken starts the next Shaken, and one that ended Inspired starts Confident; otherwise morale returns to Steady.

A small ▲ or ▼ on a counter shows its morale; the unit’s card shows the step, its traits, and why it moved.

Rare moments

The game knows how likely every result was: the unlikely ones are told, and can earn a unit a trait.

Every fight has a full spread of outcomes in its forecast, so the game knows how likely a result was. A result the dice gave 15% or less is Notable, 5% or less Rare (the camera comes in), 1% or less Legendary (the screen is yours). When one of your units earns a trait by it, the trait is stamped on its card, with the moment under it, in place of the banner.

Everyday news comes as a field dispatch: a torn telegram drops in and a rubber stamp lands on it (TAKEN, LOST, HELD, RARE). The biggest moments (a legendary deed, a ring closing, the last turns) come as a newsreel title card.

The moments: Unscathed (an attack that should have cost heavily cost nothing), Turned the Tables (a defender took far more than it lost), Held Alone (3 strength or less, through 3 attacks in a turn), Giant Killer (a unit two grades better or twice the cost), Breakout (a ring broken), Vengeance (a Nemesis destroyed), At the Last (an objective taken on the last turn), Hot Streak (kills on three turns running), and a Rally (a shaken unit winning).

One legendary moment, or two rare ones of a kind, earns that moment’s trait: Charmed (+1 initiative), Hard Nut (+1 defence with two enemies beside it), Iron Nerve (the first attack each turn does not wear it down), Giant Killers (+2 attack against Veteran units and better), Ring-Breakers (through one hex of the enemy’s zone of control), Avengers (+2 attack against a unit that has struck its side since its last turn), Closers (+1 attack in the last three turns). A Rally earns the Rallied ribbon.

Hot Streak makes a unit Bloodied at once: +1 attack, but the enemy goes for it first, and whoever destroys a Bloodied unit takes a bounty of 100 prestige.

An enemy unit that hurts one of yours twice becomes its Nemesis, marked on the map. Destroying it is vengeance; destroying the unit that killed one of your medal holders is legendary.

A unit keeps three earned traits. A fourth asks, at headquarters, which to let go. Morale’s own four are not counted.

Weather

Bad weather slows armies, blinds guns and grounds aircraft, and favours whoever is dug in.

Bad weather slows armies, blinds guns and aircraft, and leaves tanks and guns stuck in mud easier to hit. The tables show each type of unit.

Roads never slow ground units. Artillery and aircraft lose most in attack, so bad weather favours whoever is holding a town, a fort or a dug-in line: their protection is never reduced.

Mud and storm keep aircraft on the ground, except on a city’s runways: from a city they take off, in a storm at a point more per hex and with less attack. One that lands away from a city stays down while the weather lasts.

Rain, snow and mud cut every unit’s sight by a hex, a storm by two. Mud, snow and storm shorten supply off the roads.

Winter Gear lifts the snow’s slowing for the rest of the battle; Mud Engineers lift rain and mud for three turns; Clear Skies clears the weather for two turns, for both sides.

Supply

Supply runs from your map edge and the towns you hold; a unit cut off from it wastes away.

Supply runs 6 hexes across country from your map edge and 12 along a road; mud, snow and storms shorten it off the roads. Towns you hold pass it on 4 hexes (8 by road) while they are connected to your edge. Railheads, the rail junctions, supply as far as your edge in any weather.

Enemy units block supply, and so do the hexes next to them unless your own unit stands there. Enemy aircraft slow it. A unit out of supply loses strength every turn and cannot refit.

Surround a city or fort and it is besieged. Its garrison lives on stores first: 3 turns in an objective city or a fort, 2 in another city. Then it is out of supply, loses Dig Deep and 2 points of its defence every turn, and from the siege’s third turn can no longer stay dug in.

Break the ring and supply comes back at once. Opening a corridor into a pocket pays prestige, cheap refits and experience. The Airlift tactic supplies one pocket for a turn.

Every town or airfield you hold pays 5 prestige at the start of each of your turns, and every objective 15, from the second turn on. The enemy’s towns pay the enemy. The battle dossier shows what yours pay.

Tap the supply button under the status bar for the supply view: shaded ground has no supply, hatching marks the front (in supply, but too close to the enemy to refit), a cross in a disc marks a worn unit that can refit where it stands (brass at a discount), dashed lines run to your towns, rail discs mark junctions, and tags mark stores, pockets and sieges.

Calling up units

Bring a unit into the battle, one a turn, to a town your supply reaches.

Call up a unit from the battle menu or the Supply view: one a turn, to a town your supply reaches with no enemy beside it. Choose the unit, then the town on the map. It arrives at the start of your next turn at a railhead, a turn later elsewhere.

Tap a unit’s picture or name in the list to see its numbers before you choose it.

You may have up to five units more than you began the battle with. A unit of your own army left out of the battle comes up as it is, for half its price; a new unit comes Green, a quarter dearer than at headquarters, and in a campaign stays in the army afterwards.

The computer may call up units to replace its losses and, beyond that, two more on Normal and five on Hard (none on Easy). Your opponent in a match may do all that you can.

Encirclement

Seal enemy units off from their supply and they weaken, break and surrender.

A ground unit is encircled when it has no way to trace supply back to its own edge, a railhead or a connected town. Being merely beyond supply’s reach is not enough: it must be sealed in.

Cut off two or more enemy units with one move or attack, or a garrison in a town, and you have closed a ring: the camera pulls back, the ring is drawn round the pocket, and the unit that closed it gains experience. The pocket is named for the nearest town.

A trapped garrison lives on its town’s stores first. Each turn a trapped unit begins out of supply, it loses a step of morale. At the start of its turn it surrenders if Broken, and one time in three if Shaken. An Unbreakable unit never surrenders: it has to be taken by storm.

Every unit that surrenders counts as destroyed and pays 25 prestige. A tank, gun or anti-tank gun is taken intact one time in three, worth a third of its price more, and joins your army weak and Green. It is the only way to have an enemy’s machine in your army. A captured unit can never be upgraded.

The same rules hold for your own units, and the enemy closes rings too. A red ring mark on a counter shows a unit in a pocket, with its turns of stores left; its card says where it stands. Break the ring, or reach it with supply, and the clock stops at once.

The result screen lists every pocket of the battle and what became of it.

Fog of war

You see only what your units can see; scout before you commit.

You see only what your units can: recon furthest, aircraft and the rest less, and less again in rain, snow and mud. Ground out of sight is shaded.

A move into unseen enemies stops where they are found: an ambush. Scout before you commit the armour. The enemy plays under the same fog.

Fog of war is chosen with the difficulty when a campaign or battle begins: always on for Hard, on to begin with for Normal, off to begin with for Easy. Battle Mode matches are always fought under fog; Scenario matches never are. The Intel view keeps enemies you have lost sight of where they were last seen, for two turns (longer with a commander’s Intelligence).

Map views

Battle, Supply and Intel: three ways to read the same map.

The switch under the status bar changes what the map shows. Battle is the plain map. Supply and Intel show a one-line summary; tap it for the full panel, and tap the map to fold it away.

Supply shows where your army can be supplied and refit, your supply lines and railheads, and every pocket and siege.

Intel shows each objective and who holds it, where the enemies you know of could strike next turn, how far your army can see, enemies last seen, and what victory still needs. With a commander’s Intelligence it also brings word of enemy reinforcements, and it always gives the weather for the next three turns.

Buy intelligence with prestige, each piece once a turn: aerial reconnaissance over a point you choose, agent reports from an area, a signals intercept (where the enemy is massing and its next reinforcements), the enemy’s supply, and once a battle a dossier on the enemy commander. In Battle Mode matches too, at the same prices; your opponent is not told what you bought.

Under fog an enemy’s strength shows only as a range (7–8), unless your recon is beside it or you fought it this turn or last. Destroy a unit or put it to flight and prisoners give away what else is near.

Goals and events

Optional goals pay extra; written events change a battle as it goes.

Some battles have optional goals (take a crossing early, hold a flank, destroy the enemy’s armour). Each pays prestige and seasons the unit that met it. None is needed to win, and none counts toward the stars. The battle menu lists them.

Battles may also have written events: a counter-attack, a weather front, partisans at a bridge. Each is told with a banner as it comes.

Goals and events are for battles against the computer only.

The enemy

The computer plans a main effort, masses, and strikes together; on Easy it comes piecemeal.

The computer sees what you see and rolls the same dice. Difficulty changes how well it plans, and the handicaps the setup screen names.

On Normal and Hard it chooses a main effort, gathers a few hexes short of a defended town, and goes in together once enough has arrived, or when time is running out.

It clears the units beside a town before striking the garrison, and puts its attacks on one unit at a time. Hard also goes for the guns covering the town, and picks the unit it can destroy that turn.

Defending, it sends reserves to the towns under pressure, puts its fire on the units at its gates, and counter-attacks a town you hold weakly. It also stands its flak beside its guns, aircraft and garrisons.

On Easy it marches on the nearest town and attacks piecemeal. Signals Intercept tells you where the enemy’s main effort is aimed.

Your army

Your core units go with you from battle to battle: keep them alive and they grow better.

Your core units go with you from battle to battle. Experience, shown as brass pips, makes them better at everything, from Green through Trained, Regular, Veteran and Elite to Guards: each grade adds 1 to attack and 10% to defence.

A core unit destroyed is gone for good. Pull a badly damaged unit back rather than lose it.

Lost units leave places open. At Headquarters you can buy your own choice for them; any still open when you march are filled free by High Command, with green units.

Refilling a damaged unit with recruits is free, as far as High Command’s pool of men goes (see The grind). Up to three recruits cost nothing more; each one beyond costs it a point of its progress toward the next grade, and only when that runs out does it drop a grade, the rest carried into the lower one. Veteran replacements cost prestige and keep the grade and its progress. Upgrades to newer equipment keep it too. When units are below strength, Refit the army near the top of Headquarters refills every unit at once: free with recruits, or keeping every grade by paying for veterans only where recruits would cost one (elsewhere recruits cost only progress). Short of prestige, it keeps the highest grades it can afford.

In battle, a unit out of contact and in supply can rest for a turn to regain up to three strength, for prestige.

Experience comes with hard fighting, a step at a time. A lost battle does not end the campaign; it sends it down a harder road.

Attached units are lent for one battle only.

Grades

Green, Trained, Regular, Veteran, Elite and Guards. A unit needs 4 hard-fought actions to become Trained, then 7, 11, 16 and 22 for each grade after (fewer with a commander's Training).

Unit cards

Every unit has a card that grows with it: its kills, its form, its dice and its story.

Every unit has a card that grows with it. Its edge is plain to Regular, then bronze at Veteran, silver at Elite and gold at Guards; a fallen unit’s is black.

Each enemy it destroys is painted on as a silhouette: a tank, a gun, infantry or an aircraft. Kills from before the cards kept count show as plain marks.

The form line is its last five battles: green won, brass held, red mauled (half its strength or more lost), black fell, with its winning streak.

The bars are its attack, defence, move and initiative, each against the average for its type (the tick): green is better than most of its kind.

Turn the card over for its road of battles on the map of Europe, every die it has thrown, its best and worst roll, and its luck: hits against what the odds promised. In a duel the cards also show its record against that type of enemy, and whether it has met this very unit before.

The card album, at Headquarters and on your Battle Group, holds every card, the fallen in their black frames.

A unit you lose takes a last stamp on its card: Killed in Action, with a bugle, or Taken Prisoner when its pocket surrenders. If its second chance was thrown and could not save it, the stamp says Second Chance Spent. Its epitaph tells the marks it took on the way, and a campaign’s fallen keep both in the card album and on the Memorial Wall. Watch the enemy’s turn and each mark is stamped as the blow lands.

A unit’s big moments are stamped on its card too, in place of a banner: Cheated Death (it came through odds that should have destroyed it, and keeps a second chance in every battle), Rolled Again (that second chance thrown, and spent until the next battle), and a Long Shot (an enemy destroyed against the odds). Every objective is put to the name of the unit that took it and listed on its card; its third earns the Town Taker ribbon.

Falling back

A unit of your army destroyed defending may be ordered to fall back: an even chance it reaches the lines.

When one of your army’s units (a campaign’s, or a Battle Group’s) is destroyed defending, you may order it to fall back. It leaves the map either way and cannot be called up again in that battle, but there is an even chance it reaches the lines.

One that makes it is stamped Made It Back and rejoins after the battle with 1 strength and everything it had (in Battle Mode it needs no reviving, but takes its next match with 1 strength).

It carries Regret of Living: −1 attack, −1 initiative, and no second order to fall back. It lifts when the unit destroys an enemy, or at the Rest Centre (in Battle Mode, a match sat out).

Not offered for a unit lost attacking, cut off and surrendering, or starved.

Brothers in arms and luck

Units that fight side by side bond; units that throw well are marked lucky.

Brothers in arms: two units of your army that have fought 5 battles together are bonded. Side by side, each gets +1 attack and +1 defence (once, however many partners are beside it).

If one is destroyed where its partner can see, the partner is shaken, but strikes the unit that did it 2 harder for the rest of the battle. The back of the card names its brothers, and those it is close to.

Luck: once a unit has thrown 30 dice, its card marks it Lucky (its hits 15% or more above what the odds promised) or Cursed (as far below). A mark only.

Over 60 dice and 25% above, it earns Lucky Star: +1 attack, +1 initiative.

Earned traits

Units earn traits by what they live through, never by chance, and keep them like medals.

Besides morale’s four, a unit earns traits by what it lives through, never by chance, and keeps them from battle to battle like medals. The unit’s card says how each was earned.

Tank Hunters: destroy 3 armoured units in a battle; +2 attack against armour. Street Fighters: hold a town or city through 3 attacks in a battle; +2 defence there.

Winter-Hardened: come through a battle with 5 turns of snow; snow neither slows it nor cuts its fire. Mud-Wise: the same in rain and mud.

Ambushed under fog of war, a steady unit becomes Alert (it sees a hex further); a shaken one becomes Hesitant (a hex less movement under fog, until it wins a battle).

Shell-Shocked: lose 6 strength between two of its turns; −1 initiative, and repeated attacks wear it down twice as fast, until it takes replacements or the battle ends.

Battle-Weary: in a campaign, a unit worn out (wear 8 of 10); −1 attack and movement until rest brings its wear down to 3. See Hardships.

In matches against another player, earned traits are in Battle Mode only.

Hardships

Units take hardships from what happens to them, and each has a way back.

War is mostly a hard struggle. Units take hardships from what happens to them, and each has a way back: beat one by a unit’s own deeds and its card earns Back from the Brink. Hardships never take one of the three kept-trait places.

In battle, any unit, the enemy’s too: Gun-Shy (cut to 2 strength or less and lived: −2 attack against a stronger unit, until it destroys an enemy). Ran Once (forced back twice in a battle: −1 defence, until it holds through 3 attacks). Air-Shy (bombed 3 times: −1 defence against aircraft, until one is brought down near it). Pocket Fever (3 turns cut off: −1 attack and defence while cut off, until its side closes a ring near it). Frostbitten (3 turns of snow out of supply: −1 attack and defence in snow). Glory-Hungry (3 kills in a battle: +1 attack, −2 defence, and the enemy goes for it). Grieving (its brother in arms destroyed: −1 attack and initiative, until it destroys a unit of the killer’s kind, or three battles pass).

Between battles, the campaign army: Rusty (sat out two battles in a row with nothing to recover from: −1 attack and initiative, until it fights). Worn Tracks (armour and recon worn to 6: −1 movement). Overconfident (5 wins in a row: −1 defence, until a battle is lost). Ill-Starred (dice a quarter under the odds over 60 throws: −1 initiative, until it lands a long shot). Green Blood (more than half its men new recruits: −1 initiative, until its first kill).

Command and discipline: Reprimanded (forced off an objective: −1 initiative and no medals, until it takes an objective or holds one through a battle won; a second reprimand is a demotion). Leaderless (6 or more strength lost in one blow, its officer fallen: −1 initiative and morale no higher than Steady, until a battle at rest or veterans at a refit). Desertions (a lost battle ended Broken or cut off: 2 strength gone, then 1 after each battle, until a win or the Mess Hall). Sullen (sent in worn out, wear 8 or more: morale falls twice as fast, −1 attack when Shaken or worse, until the Mess Hall).

Demoted: a unit that ends a lost battle Broken, or is reprimanded twice in one battle, drops a grade and its progress starts again.

A battle at rest (leave a unit Staying) lifts Glory-Hungry and Leaderless and takes 5 off its wear. The rest need Headquarters: the Rest Centre for Gun-Shy, Ran Once and Air-Shy; the Mess Hall for Sullen and Desertions; the Infirmary for Pocket Fever and Frostbitten (the Motor Pool for a machine); the Training Ground for Rusty and Green Blood.

In Battle Mode, hardships come only in battle; a Battle Group carries none between matches except wear: its units wear down match after match as an army does, worn armour takes Worn Tracks into a match, and a unit left out of a match rests.

Medals and names

Remarkable deeds earn medals with small permanent bonuses; every unit has a name of its own.

A unit of your army that does something remarkable in battle is decorated, under its own army’s name for the medal: an Iron Cross, a Military Cross, a Silver Star, an Order of the Red Star.

Each medal gives a small permanent bonus, kept through refits and upgrades: destroying four enemy units in a battle earns +1 attack; surviving five attacks in one enemy turn, +1 defence; taking the first objective of a decisive victory, +1 movement. Tap a decorated unit’s ribbons at Headquarters to see its medals and what it can still win.

In a unit’s dossier every number shows where it comes from: its base, each medal, and its experience.

Every unit joins with a name of its own, in its army’s voice and fitting its kind: no two in an army alike, and never a fallen unit’s. Tap a unit’s name at Headquarters to change it, or leave it empty for another.

Headquarters

Between battles your army waits in camp: buy, refit, heal, rest and train it, with never enough for everyone.

Between battles your army waits in a camp behind the lines. Tap a building to see what it does and who needs it, or tap a unit along the foot of the camp and then a building to send it there.

Recruiting Office: three candidates each visit, each with a trait or a flaw, priced to match; every other unit of the war at its list price; refits, veterans and upgrades on each unit. Tap a unit’s picture or name in the list to see its numbers before you buy.

Infirmary (2 beds) and Motor Pool (2 bays): men and machines back to 80% strength without recruits, keeping their progress; the Motor Pool also takes away all wear, Worn Tracks, Battle-Weary and a machine’s Pocket Fever. A unit there misses the next battle.

Rest Centre (2 cots): a 65% chance to lift Gun-Shy, Ran Once, Air-Shy, Fearful, Hesitant or Shell-Shocked. Cured or not, the unit misses the next battle.

Mess Hall (3 seats) and Training Ground (2 places) act at once and the unit still fights: a hot meal lifts Sullen and Desertions, eases grief and sends a Shaken unit in Steady; drill lifts Rusty and Green Blood and gives up to 3 points toward the next grade, never the grade itself.

Rations are for the whole army: short (saves 30 prestige, every unit goes in a step lower), standard, or extra (60 prestige, a step higher).

Every building costs prestige and has only a few places. There is never enough for everyone: choose who gets care, and who goes as they are.

Each side keeps its own camp: a requisitioned village for the Germans, an English manor’s grounds for the Western Allies (an oasis in North Africa), log dugouts in the forest for the Soviets. Its sky is the season of the coming battle’s country: rain, mud, snow, a storm, or a grey day with the sun breaking through. The weather is only the camp’s; the battle keeps its own.

The camp is alive: rifles crack on the range, the mess hall murmurs and steams, sparks fly in the workshop, a typewriter clatters, crows cross the sky by day and a truck’s lights by night. Scroll and the sounds move with what is in front of you; they are louder where you have sent units.

The grind

In a campaign men are scarce and units wear out: rest and care decide who can fight.

In a campaign men are scarce. After each battle High Command sends a pool of replacements: more for a decisive win, fewer for a defeat, and fewer still for Germany as the war goes on (more for the Allies). Recruits come only from it, the best units first, as far as it goes; the rest stay short. Veterans can still be bought with prestige, and in a battle reinforcements still cost prestige.

When the army falls below half its strength, High Command drafts men (counting those already waiting) to bring it back to a little over half, so a bad run never ends a war by itself.

Wear (0 to 10): each battle a unit fights adds 2 (1 when the whole army fought, no one to spare), a mauling 1 more. A battle at rest takes 5 off. At 6, armour and recon get Worn Tracks; at 8, any unit is Battle-Weary, until rest brings it down to 3.

A unit that ends a battle at half strength or less is mauled: no men reach it until it has been in the Infirmary (men) or the Motor Pool (machines), which bring it back to 80% after a battle out. Beds and bays are few; a mauled unit with none waits, or goes into battle as it is.

Headquarters shows the army’s condition: who is short, worn, mauled or carrying a hardship, and what would help.

The fallen

Fallen medal holders get a send-off, a wreath, and a page on the Roll of Honour.

A unit with a medal that falls gets the full send-off: its card, its medals laid down one by one, and a wreath where it fell for the rest of the battle. Storied units have the card too.

Take back the hex where one of your wreathed units fell and the unit that does it tends to the dead: its morale rises a step.

After the battle each fallen medal holder has a page on the Roll of Honour, with an epitaph written from what it did. Headquarters keeps every unit lost on the Memorial Wall.

A new unit of the same kind can be raised in a fallen medal holder’s name: “II”, then “III”. It starts a grade up and keeps one of the fallen unit’s traits. Each name is carried once, and a fallen unit’s own name is never used again.

The Victory Parade ends with the fallen: they did not march today.

The end of a campaign

Win and your army parades; win or lose, you hand out honours and choose who is remembered.

Win a campaign and your army marches in a Victory Parade: the road you took, your general’s order of the day, every unit that came through, the Roll of Honour of those lost, and a citation to share. The surviving unit that destroyed most is named Unit of the Campaign and wins the Campaign Star. Lose a campaign and your general gives a last order instead, before the Roll of Honour and the record of the war. Your commander’s profile keeps every campaign, won or lost, to watch again; each finale can be made into a film to share.

The parade is your army’s own cards: go through them at your own pace, or Let them march; turn a card over for its road, battle by battle, with the marks each battle gave and took.

Then the honours are yours to give: Commander’s Choice to any unit, Back from the Brink to one that beat a hardship, Brothers in Arms to a bonded pair. Tap a name on the Roll of Honour to lay a wreath and choose its epitaph.

After the war most units go home. Up to three can stand in the Hall of Honour for good (in your commander’s profile), and one can join your Battle Group: at Trained, keeping its name, medals, traits and story. Lose the war and up to three units can get out, to the Hall. What you settle is kept for good and cannot be changed.

Your commander

You are the commander: one career across every battle, with attributes and tactics to choose.

You are the commander: one career across every campaign and Quick Battle. Every battle brings experience (more for a decisive victory, more on Hard) and Command Points (1 for fighting, 2 for a marginal victory, 3 for a decisive one).

Each level gives an attribute point. Attributes strengthen the whole army: Aggression and Resolve add attack and defence, Mobility speed, Logistics cheaper refits, Training faster promotions, Intelligence longer sight.

Command Points unlock tactics: battle tactics like an Artillery Barrage, unit tactics like Rally, weather tactics like Winter Gear. Take as many into battle as you have slots (one more at levels 5, 10, 15 and 20) and use them from the Tactics button.

The enemy commander grows with you — about half your level on Easy, level with you on Normal, ahead of you on Hard — and uses tactics of its own.

Ranks

Levels 1 to 20: Major (1–2); Colonel (3–5); Brigadier (6–9); Major General (10–13); Lieutenant General (14–17); General (18–19); Field Marshal (20).

Postings and the Field Manual

Your commander studies and trains while the game is closed.

Your commander works while the game is closed, up to a day at a time. Choose a posting on the Commander page.

Train troops gives your commander 5 experience an hour, and drills one Green Battle Group unit you choose: a point of experience every 2 hours, up to Trained. Command Points can train a unit no further than that either: anything better is earned in battle. Campaign armies learn only in battle.

Switch on Study reminders in Settings for a notification when a chapter is learned, and add the Panzerkeil widget to your Home Screen to see your commander's progress at a glance.

Stars and the operations

Three stars a battle, a daily and a weekly operation, and a score for every campaign.

Every battle offers three stars: win it, win it decisively, and lose none of your units.

The main menu offers two operations, the same for every player: one short battle each day and one each week, each with a twist (a blizzard, a veteran enemy, fewer turns). Your best score goes to that day's or week's Game Center leaderboard.

Win the Daily Operation day after day to build a streak. A new one begins at 08:00 UTC, midnight on the US west coast.

Each campaign keeps a score, shown at Headquarters and on its own leaderboard. Every battle adds its stars, speed, enemy destroyed and army kept. Battles further into the campaign are worth more, up to double; Hard pays double and Easy about a third. A longer road through the campaign earns no more: only your best battles count, as many as the road of decisive victories has. A win your side never had in the real war pays a quarter more, and winning the campaign adds a bonus.

Campaigns grow harder as they go, and battles your side lost in the real war are harder than those it won.

Matches

Play any battle against another player through Game Center, a turn at a time.

Play any battle against another player through Game Center: invite a friend or let Game Center find an opponent. Each of you commands one side. A Scenario match is a battle as written; Battle Mode brings your own army (see Battle Mode).

Take your turn whenever you like and tap End Turn; your opponent gets a notification. Opening a match replays their last turn before you move. A turn not taken in time (24 hours unless you chose otherwise) passes over.

Leave a match at any time with Back to Matches (on the Waiting banner, or in the ☰ menu); moves you have made are kept until you tap End Turn. The Multiplayer button on the title screen shows how many matches are waiting for your move.

Each commander brings the tactics they have chosen; attribute bonuses are off, so the battle is decided by play. Swipe a match in the list to resign or ask for a rematch.

History rarely made a fair fight, so each battle is labelled. Even battles favour neither side. In the others one side is Favoured and the other is a Challenge, which pays 1 extra Command Point for a win, or a Hard Challenge, which pays 1 extra for playing it through and 2 more for a win. Resigning earns none of this, nor does letting the clock play most of your turns. Find an Opponent draws your side at random; when you invite a friend you can choose.

Tap Chat to talk with your opponent at any time, during your turn or theirs (while you wait, Chat sits beside Back to Matches). Your opponent gets a notification; set messages like “Good luck!” are a tap away. Messages are filtered; touch and hold one to report it, or block the player from the chat’s menu. Chat can be turned off in Settings. A match begun on an older version of the game has set messages only; Chat then opens those and says why.

If your opponent has been quiet for a while, swipe the match and tap Nudge to remind them, once every 12 hours. The match list shows the time left on each turn, your record against each opponent, and a rematch button once a match is over.

A finished match shows once in your list with its result, then goes on your record: tap Your Record at the bottom of the list for your results against each opponent and the full history. Wins also count on the Multiplayer leaderboard.

Every turn your opponent sends is checked by playing it again on your own copy of the battle. If one does not check out, the match stops and is marked Disputed; the banner says why, and Details for a report gives the figures to send us. The mark goes, and the result counts again, once a later turn checks out.

Battle Mode

Fight matches with your own Battle Group: an army that grows, falls and is revived from match to match.

Battle Mode: you fight with your own Battle Group, one for each side of the war: tap its banner under Your Battle Groups on the Multiplayer screen. Name the army and give it an emblem; your opponent sees its name, rank, record and best three units before the battle, and you see theirs. Setting up a match, you choose your side and the weather (the season's, or one kind for the whole battle); your opponent moves first.

Battle Mode matches play the newest rules (the air war, hardships, falling back), always under fog of war. Both players need the newest version.

Each unit has its own page: tap its tile. Titles come with battles fought (Blooded, Seasoned, Hardened, Old Hands, Legends) and with feats (Tank Killers, Sky Hunters, Spearhead, The Wall, Unbroken). Every battle won adds an honour to each unit that fought it. Titles and honours are for show; medals are what change a unit in battle.

Your army has a camp of its own, at the top of its screen: the Axis camp, or for the Allies the East’s dugouts when most of the army is Soviet and the West’s huts otherwise, under the season’s sky. Recruit and re-equip at the Recruiting Office (tap a unit there to see its numbers before you pay); revive squads and crews in the Infirmary and machines in the Motor Pool; train with Command Points or choose who drills on the Training Ground; stand worn units down at the Rest Centre to rest in reserve; and hear the army’s records and rivals in the Mess Hall. Each does its work at once: there are no beds to wait for in Battle Mode.

After a match, a short ceremony shows who was promoted, who earned a title, a feat or a medal, and who fell. A unit destroyed in battle keeps its grade, medals and record, but stays at headquarters until you revive it for Command Points (half what it cost to raise, a little more the better it is): in the Infirmary or the Motor Pool of your army's camp, or from its tile's orders. Share a unit's card from its page. Touch and hold a tile for its orders, or pin a favourite to the front.

The same banners are on your commander's profile: tap one to open it, or raise the side you have not yet. Tap your best unit on the title screen to open its page.

Colour and text size

Colours for colour-blind players, and larger text.

If red and green, or blue and yellow, are hard to tell apart, choose Colour vision in Settings: the map, its keys and the combat odds change to colours you can tell apart. Enemy counters always carry a dark pennant in the corner, whatever the colours.

For larger text, choose Text size in Settings. It adds to the text size set for your device in its Settings app; town names, strengths and odds on the map grow a little too.

Older matches

A match keeps the rules it began with, so both players always play the same game.

A Scenario match (a battle as written) plays the rules every player has had for some time: morale, manoeuvre, weather, encirclement and Cheated Death, but no hardships, no falling back and no air war, and an overrun throws three more dice, not one.

Battle Mode plays the newest rules, so both players need the newest version.

A match begun on an older version goes on under the rules it began with, so a player who has not yet updated can finish it. The oldest of them play without some of what these pages describe: morale, manoeuvre, the newer weather or encirclement.
