Your Army

Not a box of counters: an army of your own, named, decorated and remembered, that is never quite the same twice.

In Panzerkeil the army is yours. Your units carry their names, grades, medals, traits and scars from battle to battle, and in Battle Mode you raise an army of your own from every unit of the war. This page sets out all of it, from the game's own rules.

Battle to battle

Your campaign army

The units that start a campaign with you are your core. What happens to them in one battle follows them into the next.

Carried through the war

Your core units go with you from battle to battle with their strength, grade, medals, traits and kills. A core unit destroyed is gone for good, so pull a badly damaged one back rather than lose it.

Named and marked

Tap a unit's name at Headquarters to give it a name of your own, choose an insignia for its counter, and paint the army seasonal, factory, winter, desert or ambush. Names and insignia follow a unit through refits and upgrades.

Refit and re-equip

Refilling a damaged unit with recruits is free, but replacing four or more strength costs it a grade; veteran replacements cost prestige and keep the grade. Re-equipping with the next type in its line keeps its grade, name and medals. Refit the army fills every unit at once.

Buy, call up, bring up

Buy units at Headquarters for the places a battle has, and up to 5 more to hold in reserve. In battle, bring a reserve unit up for half its price, or call up a new one to a town your supply reaches.

Unit of the Battle, Unit of the Campaign

Each battle's report names its distinguished unit, the one that destroyed most. Win a campaign and the survivor that destroyed most over the whole war is named Unit of the Campaign and wins the Campaign Star.

Service records and the Roll of Honour

Every unit keeps a service record: when it joined, every battle with its result, kills, promotions and medals, every re-equipping, its escapes and its long shots. The fallen go on the Roll of Honour, shown at the end of the war.

Battle Mode

Battle Groups

For matches against other players: an army you raise yourself, one for each side of the war, that always comes back.

Raise it

Each commander keeps one Axis and one Allied Battle Group. A group starts with three units of the nation you choose (Axis: Germany, Italy and Romania; Allied: United States, United Kingdom, Soviet Union, France, Poland, Greece and Yugoslavia), and grows to 30 units of any nation on its side. Name the army and give it an emblem.

Buy it with Command Points

The same Command Points as your tactics buy any unit of the war: 1 for every 100 of its price, at least 1, plus 1 for every two years after 1939. A grade costs its number in points, up to Trained, and re-equipping with the next of a line costs the difference, at least 1.

Take the field

Both sides field 2,800 points, so a smaller army is filled out with green units from the computer, which you command. Grades raise a unit's worth (Green ×1, Trained ×1.1, Regular ×1.15, Veteran ×1.25, Elite ×1.6, Guards ×1.8), each medal adds 5%, and a unit ahead of the battle's year costs 25% more for each year.

It always comes back

After a match every unit returns. Survivors keep their grade, progress and medals; a unit destroyed in a defeat loses a grade. While you are away, with your commander on the Train troops posting, one unit at a time can drill: a point of experience every 2 hours, up to Trained.

Ranks and rivals

Your stronger group sets your rank: Recruit under 1,000 points, Regular under 2,800, Veteran from there, and Game Center pairs players of the same rank. The army screen keeps its records (longest serving, most destroyed, most decorated, best single battle, most honours) and your score against each rival.

The army card

Before a match your opponent sees your army's name, emblem, rank, record and best three units, and you see theirs. Share any unit's card from its page.

Titles, feats and honours

Titles come with battles fought: Blooded (1 battle), Seasoned (5 battles), Hardened (10 battles), Old Hands (18 battles), Legends (25 battles).

  • Tank Killers: 10 tanks destroyed.
  • Sky Hunters: 10 aircraft destroyed.
  • Spearhead: First into a town in 3 battles.
  • The Wall: Came through 5 attacks in one enemy turn.
  • Unbroken: Held on at 2 strength or less.

Every battle won adds an honour to each unit that fought it, and a ground won again counts ×2, ×3 and on. After a match a short ceremony shows who was promoted, who earned a title, a feat or a medal, and who lost a grade. Titles and honours are for show: medals are what change a unit in battle.

Nerve

Morale

Every unit has morale, aircraft too, and nothing predicts it.

Surprises move it

Morale moves only when a fight goes clearly better or worse than its odds, by 2.5 strength points or more against the forecast. Hit harder and lose less than expected, and a unit rises a step while the enemy it fought falls one. Driven out of a town, a unit falls a step.

It spreads

A surprise touches each unit beside it half the time, the same way. At the start of its turn a unit drifts a step toward its neighbours when most of them stand above or below it.

A fresh start each battle

Morale returns to Steady for each battle (Shaken, for a Fearful unit). Traits stay. A unit's card shows its step, its traits, and every reason it moved.

Broken and cut off

A trapped unit loses a step of morale each turn it begins out of supply. At the start of its turn it surrenders if Broken, and one time in three if Shaken; an Unbreakable unit never surrenders. A surrender pays the captor 25 prestige, and a tank, gun or anti-tank gun is taken intact one time in three and joins the captor's army Green at 3 strength.

Fate and long odds

Cheating death

Cheated Death

A unit that comes through an attack with a 60% chance or worse of being destroyed has Cheated Death, for good. With it comes a second chance in every battle: once a battle, dice that would destroy it are thrown again, and the second throw stands. Your army keeps it from battle to battle.

Against the Odds

Destroy an enemy when the dice gave less than a 20% chance and the moment is celebrated, the odds counting down to the real chance; under 8% it is the Impossible Shot, a bigger celebration still. The unit wins the Long Shot ribbon, ×2, ×3 and on for each one after, with its slimmest odds. A record only: it changes no dice.

The fate moment

When an enemy attack has a good chance of destroying one of your units, the turn slows to show it, and the more history the unit has (battles, kills, medals, a name of its own), the bigger the moment. Your own attacks with a good chance of a kill are framed close up.

Your general's voice

A general of your side calls the moments aloud: a town taken or lost, a ring closed, a storied unit fallen, a unit cheating death, a long shot, the last turns.
