The real battle

Crossing the Rhine

22 March to 18 April 1945

American troops had seized the bridge at Remagen on 7 March 1945. Patton crossed at Oppenheim on 22 March. The next night Montgomery's Operation Plunder crossed at Wesel, and a huge airborne drop followed the next morning. The two arms of the advance met at Lippstadt on 1 April, surrounding the Ruhr, the industrial heart of Germany.

The cost. About 317,000 German troops captured in the Ruhr pocket, which surrendered by 18 April.

The result. The Allies won.

Key places

  • Remagen taken by the Allies on 7 March 1945
  • Wesel taken by the Allies on 24 March 1945
  • Lippstadt taken by the Allies on 1 April 1945
  • Essen taken by the Allies on 10 April 1945

In the game, when the battle ends, your result is set beside this one: who held each town and when, the account and the cost, read aloud by the general of your side.

The Western Allies · stage 12 of 13 · 1945

As the Western Allies: Crossing the Rhine

The last great barrier before Germany is the Rhine. Upstream at Remagen the Americans have already seized a bridge, by luck and nerve; downstream at Wesel the river is to be crossed in strength, with airborne divisions dropped on the far bank. Take both bridgeheads, break out, and close a ring around the Ruhr at Lippstadt. The industrial heart of Germany is the prize, and its fall will end the war in the west.
