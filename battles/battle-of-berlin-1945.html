The Battle of Berlin

16 April to 2 May 1945. In Panzerkeil you fight it from either side, as the Wehrmacht or as the Red Army. Below: what really happened, and how the game's version plays.

The real battle

The Battle of Berlin

16 April to 2 May 1945

The Red Army attacked over the Oder on 16 April 1945 and took the Seelow Heights after three days of costly assaults. By 25 April Berlin was surrounded, and the old men and boys of its last defence were fighting in the streets. Hitler killed himself on 30 April. The city surrendered on 2 May, and Germany on 8 May.

The cost. About 81,000 Soviet and Polish dead. Perhaps 100,000 German soldiers dead, and as many as 125,000 civilians.

The result. The Allies won.

Key places

  • Seelow taken by the Allies on 19 April 1945 (the Heights)
  • Tempelhof taken by the Allies on 26 April 1945
  • Potsdam taken by the Allies on 27 April 1945
  • Reichstag taken by the Allies on 2 May 1945

In the game, when the battle ends, your result is set beside this one: who held each town and when, the account and the cost, read aloud by the general of your side.

In Panzerkeil

Fight it yourself

Both sides of this battle are in the game: fight it in each campaign, or in Quick Battle, or against another player in a match, one of you on each side.

The Axis Campaign · stage 16 of 17 · 1945

As the Wehrmacht: Die Schlacht um Berlin

Two and a half million men are on the Oder. Between them and the capital lie the Seelow Heights, the last high ground in the east, and behind the heights a city of rubble garrisoned by boys and old men. Zhukov comes straight up the highway from Küstrin; Konev is swinging in from the south; a third army will come down from the north. There is no reserve and no relief. Hold the Heights. Hold the government quarter at the Reichstag, the airfield at Tempelhof, and Potsdam. Every day the city stands is a day for the columns of refugees still moving west.

The Red Army · stage 12 of 12 · 1945

As the Red Army: Berlin

The last battle. A million men and twenty thousand guns stand on the Oder, and Berlin is sixty kilometres away. The Seelow Heights must be stormed first, then Tempelhof and Potsdam, and finally the Reichstag itself. The red banner is to fly over it by the First of May.
