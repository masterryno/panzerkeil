The Moscow Counter-Offensive

The real battle

The Moscow counter-offensive

5 December 1941 to 20 January 1942

On 5 December, with the German army exhausted and frozen outside Moscow, the Red Army counter-attacked on a wide front. Klin and Kalinin were retaken in mid-December. Hitler ordered his troops to stand fast and dismissed several senior generals. By January the Germans had been pushed back as much as 250 kilometres, but the line did not break.

The cost. About 370,000 Soviet casualties in the counter-offensive. Well over 100,000 German casualties, many from frostbite.

The result. The Allies won.

Key places

  • Klin taken by the Allies on 15 December 1941
  • Kalinin taken by the Allies on 16 December 1941
  • Kaluga taken by the Allies on 30 December 1941
  • Mozhaisk taken by the Allies on 20 January 1942

In the game, when the battle ends, your result is set beside this one: who held each town and when, the account and the cost, read aloud by the general of your side.

The Red Army · stage 3 of 12 · 1941

As the Red Army: The Winter Counter-Offensive

Forty degrees of frost. The German army at the gates of Moscow is frozen in summer uniforms, its tanks will not start and its men have not slept indoors for weeks. The fresh divisions from Siberia are ready. Strike now, everywhere: retake Klin and Kalinin in the north, Kaluga in the south, and drive the invader back from the capital. Do not stop to besiege the strongpoints: go round them.
