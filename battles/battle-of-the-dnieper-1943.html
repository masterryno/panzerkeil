The Battle of the Dnieper

August to December 1943. In Panzerkeil you fight it from either side, as the Wehrmacht or as the Red Army. Below: what really happened, and how the game's version plays.

The real battle

The Battle of the Dnieper

August to December 1943

After Kursk, the Germans fell back behind the Dnieper and called it the Panther Line. It was never finished. Soviet troops crossed the river on rafts and in small boats on a wide front from late September, holding bridgeheads under fire. Kiev was freed on 6 November. A German counter-attack retook Zhitomir, but the line had been broken for good.

The cost. Well over a million Soviet casualties. German losses are uncertain, but ran to hundreds of thousands.

The result. The Allies won.

Key places

  • Dnepropetrovsk taken by the Allies on 25 October 1943
  • Kiev taken by the Allies on 6 November 1943
  • Zhitomir taken by the Allies on 12 November 1943 (lost again, freed 31 December)
  • Cherkassy taken by the Allies on 14 December 1943

In the game, when the battle ends, your result is set beside this one: who held each town and when, the account and the cost, read aloud by the general of your side.

In Panzerkeil

Fight it yourself

Both sides of this battle are in the game: fight it in each campaign, or in Quick Battle, or against another player in a match, one of you on each side.

The Axis Campaign · stage 11 of 17 · 1943

As the Wehrmacht: Die Panther-Stellung

After Kursk the whole front has come back four hundred kilometres to the Dnieper, the last great barrier before Poland. The river is wide, the west bank is high, and for once the ground favours the defence. But the army that reached it is exhausted, and the Red Army is already across in a dozen places, on rafts and fishing boats, digging in under the bluffs. Hold Kiev, Cherkassy and Dnepropetrovsk on the river, and the depot at Zhitomir behind them. Destroy the bridgeheads while they are small. They will not stay small.

The Red Army · stage 9 of 12 · 1943

As the Red Army: Battle of the Dnieper

After Kursk the enemy has fallen back to the Dnieper, the great river of the Ukraine, and calls it the Eastern Wall. The west bank is high and the river is wide. But the army is already across in a dozen places, on rafts and fishing boats. Widen the bridgeheads, liberate Kiev, and take Cherkassy, Dnepropetrovsk and the railway at Zhitomir before the wall can be built.
