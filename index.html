An army that remembers

Name them. Paint them.
Bring them home.

Your units travel with you from battle to battle and grow from Green recruits to Guards. Each one comes with a name of its own, wins medals with lasting bonuses, from the Iron Cross to the Victoria Cross, and earns traits from what it lives through. Two that fight five battles side by side become brothers in arms.

  • Tank Hunters
  • Street Fighters
  • Winter-Hardened
  • Shell-Shocked
  • Battle-Weary

Every unit keeps a service record. The fallen go on the Roll of Honour. At the end of a war you choose who goes into the Hall of Honour; win it and your army marches in a victory parade, lose it and the last of them make a stand.

Every unit is a card

A card that
lives its war

Every unit carries a trading card that changes as it fights. The edge turns from bronze to silver to gold, kill marks line up under the picture, and the back keeps its road through the war, its form and its luck with the dice.

When something big happens, it is stamped on the card where it happened: a trait earned under fire, a third town taken, death cheated. A unit can end the war Lucky, or Cursed.

  • Town Taker
  • Lucky Star
  • Brothers in arms
  • Killed in action

Morale and the fate of a unit

One blow can
break a line

Units have morale: a surprise against the odds can shake a whole line. Trapped units starve and surrender, and closing a ring is a moment to remember. When a veteran’s life hangs on one blow, the battle slows to watch.

And the war wears them down. Units come out of battle worn, short of men and carrying hardships, from Gun-Shy to Frostbitten, that only rest, a deed or the camp can lift.

Cheated DeathA veteran that lives through odds that should have destroyed it.
Long ShotA kill against long odds, stamped on its card.
Fall BackOverrun while defending? Order it back: an even chance it reaches the lines.
Regret of LivingThe price of making it back, carried until it fights its way clear.

Headquarters

Between battles,
the camp.

Your army comes back to a camp of its own, painted for its side and its season, alive with smoke, mess-tin murmur and distant guns. There are never enough beds: choose who heals first.

Recruiting Office

New recruits and veteran replacements, refits and new equipment.

Infirmary

Two beds for the men who came back short.

Motor Pool

Two bays for mauled tanks and guns. The rest wait their turn.

Mess Hall

Short, standard or extra rations, and the talk of the camp.

Rest Centre

For the shaken and the grieving. Most come out of it themselves again.

Training Ground

Every attack is a duel

Read the odds.
Roll the dice.

Before you commit, the forecast shows attack and defence, every modifier and the losses each side can expect.

Then the two units meet card to card and every die is thrown in front of you, the side with the initiative first. Hits light up red; the one that decides it, you will remember.

Watch the enemy’s turn play out the same way, with your general on the radio calling the big moments.
